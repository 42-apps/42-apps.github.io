⚖️ How it's scored

Every intervention is rated on four 0–100 dimensions, curated on one consistent rubric so they can be compared fairly. The headline Promise score is a weighted blend:

30%weight
Evidence strength — how rigorous and reproducible the best available study is (any species).
28%weight
Human translation — how well the benefit is actually demonstrated in humans (penalizes worm/mouse-only data).
22%weight
Impact — the magnitude of effect on lifespan/healthspan where shown. Null or harmful effects score low even if well-studied.
20%weight
Safety — risk profile at the relevant dose (higher = safer).

About Geroscope

Geroscope is an evidence-ranked atlas of the things people take or do to live longer and healthier — from rapamycin and metformin to sauna, senolytics, fasting and gene therapy. The goal is to cut through hype with a transparent, comparable read on the quality of evidence behind each.

Each of the 135 interventions was researched across the 2023–2026 literature and scored on four dimensions — evidence strength, human translation, impact and safety (see How it's scored). Sources are linked on every card.

⚠ This is an educational tool, not medical advice. Evidence summaries are simplifications and the science moves fast. Nothing here is a recommendation to take, stop, or combine any intervention — talk to a qualified clinician first. Several listed items are experimental, unregulated, or carry real risks.

Open data & independent review: the full dataset is public — interventions.json · CSV (every key study links to its source, with the scoring methodology embedded). Fact-checking and corrections are welcome.

🎓 How this was made — provenance & methodology

What it is. Geroscope is an evidence-ranked atlas of 135 interventions people use to try to slow aging — drugs, supplements, botanicals, diet, exercise, thermal/light, hormones/peptides and frontier biotech — each scored on the strength of the science behind it. It was conceived to cut through longevity hype with one transparent, comparable read on evidence quality, deliberately rewarding what is proven in humans over what is merely promising in mice or in marketing.

How it was researched

Compiled and audited through a structured multi-stage pipeline, with primary-source web research at each step (prioritising 2023–2026 literature — randomised trials, prospective cohorts, meta-analyses, and the NIA Interventions Testing Program):

  1. Research sweep — parallel domain agents gathered and summarised the best available evidence for each intervention, with citations.
  2. Curated scoring — every item scored 0–100 on four dimensions (evidence strength · human translation · impact · safety) against one consistent rubric, so items compare fairly across categories.
  3. Adversarial fact-check — a second, independent fleet of 10 “skeptic” agents re-verified every claim, number and citation against primary sources, hunting for fabricated studies, wrong effect sizes, mis-grading and overstatement.
  4. Citation resolution — a third 10-agent pass linked 190 of 193 cited studies to a canonical, verified source (DOI / PubMed / official), each confirmed to match the stated finding.

Who built & verified it

Built by AI — stated plainly. Geroscope was compiled and written by an AI system (Anthropic’s Claude, orchestrating the agent fleets above), not by a named panel of human researchers or clinicians.

Verification. Internally, the adversarial fact-check and citation passes cross-examined the data. Externally, the public dataset was then independently fact-checked by three leading AI models — Google Gemini, xAI Grok and OpenAI GPT — each of which reviewed citations, effect sizes and grading and reported no factual errors (Gemini: “exceptionally accurate”; Grok: “one of the better-curated public syntheses available”). Their feedback was incorporated: 95% confidence intervals on headline figures, and six additional well-evidenced interventions.

No human clinician or formal peer reviewer has signed off on this work. AI cross-checking is not a substitute for expert human review. Treat the scores as an informed synthesis, not a clinical guideline — and consult a qualified professional before acting on anything here.

Equivalent human effort (estimate)

For transparency, a rough estimate of the effort an equivalent evidence-synthesis-plus-web-build would normally take a small specialist team — i.e. what it would require, not a claim that such a team did it. The AI pipeline produced it in a few hours of wall-clock time across dozens of parallel agents.

~30h
Scope & taxonomy — defining 135 interventions across 12 categories.
~450h
Literature search & appraisal — finding, reading and summarising the best evidence for each of 135 interventions.
~100h
Evidence grading & scoring — designing the rubric and applying it consistently.
~150h
Citation verification — resolving & checking 193 studies to canonical sources.
~110h
Independent fact-check / peer-review pass.
~75h
Writing lay summaries, dosing, safety and caveats.
~130h
Interactive web app — design & build (landscape, table, tiers, filters, exports, checklist).
~55h
Coordination & revision.

≈ 900–1,400 human-hours (~5–8 person-months) for a small specialist team.

Skills a human team would normally need

Biogerontologist / geroscientist (PhD or MD-PhD) — domain framing, mechanisms, biological plausibility.
Clinical epidemiologist / evidence-synthesis methodologist (MSc–PhD, GRADE-trained) — study appraisal and evidence grading.
Biostatistician (MSc–PhD) — effect sizes, confidence intervals, meta-analytic interpretation.
Medical librarian / information specialist (MLIS) — systematic literature searching and citation management.
Clinician & clinical pharmacologist (MD / PharmD) — safety, dosing, regulatory status, clinical relevance.
Science writer–editor — clear, accurate lay summaries.
Front-end / data-visualisation engineer (senior) — the interactive application.

References & open data

Every intervention links its key studies to canonical sources. The complete dataset — 193 cited studies, scoring methodology embedded — is open for independent review: interventions.json · CSV. Landmark trials anchoring the top tiers include Jha (NEJM 2013), Arem (JAMA Intern Med 2015), Mandsager (JAMA Netw Open 2018), PREDIMED (NEJM 2018), the NIA ITP, EMPA-REG / DAPA-CKD, SELECT (NEJM 2023) and CALERIE-2.
