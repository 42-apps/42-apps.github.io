Coloured by ancestry

Each region glows with the deep genetic lineage of the people who lived there — mtDNA & Y-DNA haplogroups, ancient-DNA samples and their living descendants. Watch the colours of humankind diverge.

The great migrations

Animated arcs trace every journey — out of Africa, the first sea crossing to Australia, into Ice-Age Europe, over Beringia to the Americas, and across the open Pacific to New Zealand (settled last, ~1300 CE).

🧭

Sites & discoveries

Fossils, first art, burials and breakthroughs light up at their date — Jebel Irhoud, Blombos, Lake Mungo, Denisova, Göbekli Tepe, Monte Verde and more, each with its story.

❄️

The Ice-Age world

See why people moved: ice sheets wall off the north, seas fall ~120 m to open land bridges, great lakes fill and dry, and a gauge reads the global temperature and sea level through the glacial cycles.

🧍

The other humans

Neanderthals, Denisovans and the Flores “Hobbit” appear as ranges that wink out as we spread — the cousins we lived alongside, and, for two of them, interbred with. Their DNA is still in us.

Honest about the science

Deep prehistory is uncertain and fast-moving. Every date, range and population here is an approximation, often debated; regions are coarse “where-ish” zones, not borders; and genetics are reduced to a few characteristic lineages. We open in a proposed southern-African “cradle”, but present it as one contested idea alongside the prevailing pan-African origin. The deeper the past, the fuzzier it becomes — that uncertainty is part of the story.
