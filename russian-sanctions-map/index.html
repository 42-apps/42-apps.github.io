About the Russian Sanctions Map

This map paints every country by its stance toward Moscow — green where relations are open and friendly, yellow where a country is neutral or partial, and red where it sanctions or isolates Russia. Russia itself — and, before 1991, the other Soviet republics — is shown in violet.

The story runs from the Russian Empire through the USSR to the Russian Federation. Drag the time slider or press ▶ to watch the world re-colour: the WWII Grand Alliance, the Iron Curtain falling across Europe, the 1990s thaw when almost everyone turned green, and the 2014 → 2022 re-freeze into today's record sanctions. The milestones tab and the ticks on the timeline mark 48 turning points; click any for the full story.

Each country's colour is an editorial reading of its relationship with Russia, not only its legal measures — for older eras it reflects bloc alignment (NATO vs. the Warsaw Pact, the Non-Aligned Movement). The hard cases — India, Turkey, Serbia, the Gulf, Central Asia, Armenia, Georgia — are flagged in their detail cards.

Sources: OFAC, the EU Council sanctions timeline, the UK & G7, Castellum.AI, the UN General Assembly ES-11 votes, SIPRI, Bruegel, CEPR, BOFIT and the Yale CELI list. Current stances reflect a June 2026 snapshot and involve judgement calls; corrections welcome.
