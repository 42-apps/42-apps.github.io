How to read your birth sky

🧭 The sky chart (looking up)

Imagine lying on your back and looking straight up. The centre is the zenith (directly overhead) and the rim is the horizon. N, E, S, W mark the compass around you. A body near the rim is low and near the horizon; near the centre it's high overhead. Bodies that had already set — below the horizon — fade into the dark ring outside the rim, so you can still see which way they lay.

♈ The zodiac wheel

This is the familiar horoscope wheel. The Ascendant (the sign rising on the eastern horizon) sits on the left; signs run anticlockwise. Each planet is placed at its degree of the zodiac. ☊ / ☋ are the Moon's north and south nodes — the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's path (the ecliptic). In Vedic astrology these are Rahu and Ketu. ℞ marks a planet that was retrograde.

⏳ Time & place matter

The signs of the slow planets barely change in a day, but the Ascendant and the whole sky wheel turn a full circle every 24 hours — so the birth time and place decide what was rising and overhead. If you don't know the time, hit “Time unknown”; the signs stay reliable but ignore the Ascendant and houses.

Positions are computed in the browser with the Astronomy Engine ephemeris (arc-minute accuracy), in the tropical zodiac. Time zones (including historical daylight saving) come from the IANA database; city coordinates from GeoNames.

About My Sky

As above, so below. This little observatory rebuilds the real sky for any moment and place in human history — and shows it several ways: stand under your sky in immersive 3D, with day/night colour and every planet labelled where it truly stood; read the astronomer's dome and the astrologer's wheel with the Ascendant, Midheaven and lunar nodes; or pull back to see the whole solar system from space. Save a sky for anyone and recall it in a tap.

Everything is geometry, not fortune-telling: the positions are physically accurate to about an arc-minute, drawn straight from a modern ephemeris. What you read into them is up to you.
